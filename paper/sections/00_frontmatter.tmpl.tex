\title{Injected Pre-Event Load Shaping Reveals an Accuracy and Robustness Trade-Off Between Adjusted and Synthetic-Control Demand-Response Baselines}

\begin{abstract}
Demand-response (DR) programs settle building flexibility against a customer baseline load (CBL) from an information set. An open, pre-specified, hash-frozen pipeline evaluates {{sample.n_buildings|int}} buildings at {{sample.n_sites|int}} campus-heavy sites of Building Data Genome 2 (2017 held-out year), with hot weekdays as proxy events. In the injected four-hour pre-event stress test, the day-of-adjusted PJM Interconnection rule (PJM-adj) lost its accuracy advantage over a ridge synthetic control method (SCM-ridge) at a break-even pre-event increase of {{H4.median_site_pcross|pct1}} at the median site (interval {{H4.median_site_pcross:ci_low|pct1}} to {{H4.median_site_pcross:ci_high|pct1}}). This is the median of site break-evens, all {{derived.H4.n_sites_pcross_lt_threshold|int}} below the pre-set ten percent threshold (exact sign test), for a worst-case placement covering the hours the adjustment reads; SCM-ridge is immune by construction. Under injected repeated dispatch, PJM-adj had the lower absolute error at every count in both windows, 14:00 to 18:00 and 16:00 to 20:00. Both errors grew, SCM-ridge's faster in the confirmatory window (slope difference {{H5.eta_diff|.4f}}); in the pre-specified secondary window it was inconclusive. As replications, day-of adjustment reduced hot-day error, and unadjusted PJM was more negatively biased on hot days than random weekdays. Under the signed-rank symmetry assumption, an adjusted comparison group (CG-adj) was equivalent to PJM-adj at the site median within a pre-set margin, with {{derived.H3.n_sites_outside_margin|int}} of {{sample.n_sites|int}} sites outside. All five confirmatory decisions met Holm-adjusted criteria. Choose and monitor baselines by the hours they read and expected shaping and dispatch.
\end{abstract}
