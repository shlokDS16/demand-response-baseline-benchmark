\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{A trade-off across two stress tests}\label{sec:discussion:tradeoff}

The two stress tests order the baselines oppositely, each under a stated condition. In the four-hour injected stress test, PJM-adj lost its accuracy advantage over SCM-ridge at a median site break-even pre-event increase of {{H4.median_site_pcross|pct1}}, tested with a site-level sign test. SCM-ridge does not read the shaped hours, so its immunity to the specified four-hour injection is a design property and not a finding. The break-even is a worst-case placement bound. Exploratory shaping shifted earlier so that it overlaps only the first of the three adjustment hours needed about three times the increase ({{exploratory.early_shape.a.pcross_frozenstyle.median_site|pct1}}, on the H4 basis of SCM-ridge without an increase). Shaping that touches no adjustment hour leaves PJM-adj unaffected, so PJM-adj is exposed through the hours its adjustment reads. The two-hour result is building-pooled and inconclusive (Section~\ref{sec:results:h4}).

Under injected repeated dispatch the order reverses. PJM-adj had the lower absolute error at each tested dispatch count $K$ in both event windows. In the confirmatory window its absolute error also grew more slowly, with a slope difference of {{H5.eta_diff|.4f}} per dispatch, but it still grew. In the pre-specified secondary 16:00 to 20:00 window the slope contrast was inconclusive and the building-pooled relative growth reversed, so the slope finding is stated for the 14:00 to 18:00 window only. Because $K=10$ is near the upper end of documented annual event counts \citep{kiliccote2010,nyiso2024dr}, $K=20$ to 40 are stress levels.

CG-adj is PJM-adj with a donor ratio, so it shares PJM-adj's exposure to shaping. No baseline is simply better: each information set trades hot-day accuracy against exposure to pre-event shaping and repeated dispatch.

\subsection{Settlement and market-design implications}\label{sec:discussion:design}

The overlap between the hours a rule reads and the hours a participant can shape is consistent with the 10\,\%-error thresholds across rules. For ISOs and regulators, the results suggest a design hypothesis for the tested setup. When a rule reads the hours just before dispatch, monitoring the gap between those hours and the lookback, or excluding the hours nearest the event, may reduce exposure to the specified injection. The two-hour buffer proposed in California \citep{caiso_bawg2017} is a design analogy that the stress test did not evaluate, and the exploratory early-shaping run is consistent with it but does not validate it. Practitioner units describe the injected test under worst-case placement and do not estimate program over-payment. At the median site the break-even adds pre-event energy equal to {{derived.H4.energy_offset_share.median_site|pct1}} of a ten percent shed, and PJM-adj would then pay about {{derived.H4.overpay_multiplier.median_site|.2f}} times the true shed.

Caps changed little within the assumed band: the building-pooled break-even was {{pcross.caisoA.4h|pct1}} for CAISO-adj and {{pcross.pjmA.4h|pct1}} for PJM-adj, whose adjustment is additive and uncapped \citep{pjm_manual11,pjm_attk}. A cap tuned to one dataset risks being arbitrary \citep{granderson2021}, and a larger cap leaves more room for gaming \citep{gabaldon2021}. These rules cannot tell licit pre-conditioning from gaming, and no behavior is asserted.

For dispatch, the design principle is how far back frequent events push the data a baseline reads \citep{enernoc2009naesb,caiso_bawg2017}, and the older SCM-ridge pre-period is one candidate route (Section~\ref{sec:results:h5}). Donor methods also need donors that do not take part in the event: SCM-ridge's edge at a five percent increase was lost once about {{derived.contam.q_cross_scmR_pjmA|pct0}} of its donors participated (Section~\ref{sec:results:h4}).

Aggregators and program designers should therefore monitor two metrics: the break-even pre-event increase and the slope of absolute error against dispatch count. For measurement and verification (M\&V) practice, rules should be specified in full, because implementation choices alone move estimates \citep{addy2015}.

\subsection{Held-out year and the tests H1 and H3}\label{sec:discussion:h1h3}

H1 and H2 replicate prior peak-window evidence \citep{granderson2021,coughlin2009}. The H1 and H3 verdicts differed between the 2016 development year and the 2017 held-out year. The development-year H1 estimate did not pass Holm correction, whereas the 2017 estimate did ({{H1.p_holm|.5f}}). The 2016 comparison group was not shown equivalent to PJM-adj, whereas in 2017 the decision was \emph{{{H3.decision|raw}}} (Holm p-value {{H3.p_holm|.4f}}).

H3 holds for the signed-rank location of the site log ratios, under the symmetry assumption, and not site by site: {{derived.H3.n_sites_outside_margin|int}} of {{sample.n_sites|int}} sites lay outside the margin (Section~\ref{sec:results:accuracy}). Equivalence at eleven sites is therefore fragile and partly constructed, and site-level validation should precede adoption of a comparison group.

\subsection{Computing and benchmark practice}\label{sec:discussion:computing}

A baseline is the measurement layer of a settlement system. Injecting known truth into real meters makes a settlement formula unit-testable for its implementation and its sensitivity to specified perturbations, although not for actual settlement outcomes or participant response. A hash-frozen plan with a manifest verified at run time fixes the analysis before the held-out year is scored. Unlike the related work of Section~\ref{sec:intro} and Wilcoxon comparisons without intervals \citep{jovanovic2025suscom}, the meters here are real and open, each result carries a site-level interval, and research questions are paired with threats to validity and archived code, as in Aragon et al.\ \citep{aragon2025suscom}.

Open real meters with injected dispatch simulate lookback contamination under the specified repeated-dispatch schedule, which bears on the scarcity of non-DR days under frequent dispatch that Li et al.\ name as an open problem \citep{li2026review}. The pipeline is CPU only, with a peak memory of about {{compute.peak_mem_gb|.1f}}\,GB over the full frozen pipeline run and {{compute.gpu_hours|.1f}} GPU hours. On one core, the rules and donor methods cost between {{compute.method.prev.ms_per_event|.3f}}\,ms (Prev, the cheapest) and {{compute.method.cgA.ms_per_event|.2f}}\,ms (CG-adj, the most expensive) per building-event (Supplement Table~\ref{tabS:cost}); PJM-adj takes {{compute.method.pjmA.ms_per_event|.3f}}\,ms and SCM-ridge {{compute.method.scmR.ms_per_event|.2f}}\,ms. One pass of all these methods over the {{sample.n_events.R1|int}} hot-day building-events takes about {{compute.method.all_unique.time_s_median|.0f}}\,s. The learned baselines cost orders of magnitude more, about {{compute.eval.lgbM.time_s|.0f}}\,s for the LightGBM-morn evaluation alone (Supplement Table~\ref{tabS:cost}).

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:discussion:limits}

\paragraph{Construct validity}Hot weekdays are proxy events and may already contain peak avoidance or utility DR, and the anonymized BDG2 sites give no program status. The stress tests inject curtailment and pre-event increases and do not observe behavior, so they do not validate real settlements or participant response. The realistic band is an assumption (Section~\ref{sec:results:h4}). $K$ beyond the documented range of annual event counts \citep{kiliccote2010,nyiso2024dr}, $p=0.20$ and $p=0.30$, and the constant ten percent shed are stress settings. The immunity of SCM-ridge holds only for the specified four-hour injection.

\paragraph{Internal validity}CG-adj shares PJM-adj's construction and its donors' site shocks, so part of the H3 similarity is by construction. The donor minimum of five leaves {{derived.cgA.n_events_missing_eagle|int}} Eagle events without a CG-adj value. Lamb lost {{sample.excl.Lamb.e3_flat_meter|int}} of {{sample.excl.Lamb.e1|int}} meters to the flat-meter filter and has {{sample.event_days.Lamb|int}} scored event days, so its site values rest on few buildings and days. Eligibility rules E1 and E3 read 2017 data. The first 2017 run stopped on a code error before any statistic (DEV-1) and was corrected under a second manifest, and a donor-curtailment error in the 2016 pilot was fixed before the freeze. LightGBM and the MLP were tuned by {{hpo.lgbM.trials|int}}-trial searches on 2016, pre-registered in their spaces and budget, with the listed implementation choices made after the freeze and before any 2017 evaluation of the tuned variants. The learned models are therefore not comprehensively tuned, and tuning is asymmetric because the SCM-ridge window grid was chosen on 2016.

\paragraph{Conclusion validity}Inference rests on {{sample.n_sites|int}} sites, so exact p-values cannot fall below the enumeration floor of $2^{-S}$ (one-sided) and Holm values below five times that floor. The exact signed-rank test assumes symmetric site differences under the null, so H3 is an equivalence of signed-rank location under that assumption. The following is descriptive and conditional on exchangeable sites (BDG2 is not a random sample). The one-sided 95\,\% Clopper-Pearson lower bound for the share of sites in the stated direction is {{derived.H4.prev_share_lb|pct1}} for H2 and H4 ({{derived.H4.prev_share_k|int}} of {{sample.n_sites|int}}) and {{derived.H5.prev_share_lb|pct1}} for H1 and H5 ({{derived.H5.prev_share_k|int}} of {{sample.n_sites|int}}). A distribution-free interval for one new site, with coverage of at least {{derived.H4.pred_int|pct1}}, runs from {{derived.H4.pred_int:ci_low|pct1}} to {{derived.H4.pred_int:ci_high|pct1}} for the H4 break-even. For the H5 slope difference it runs from {{derived.H5.pred_int:ci_low|.4f}} to {{derived.H5.pred_int:ci_high|.4f}} and includes the reverse sign. Holding the other four confirmatory p-values fixed, H4 would stay rejected under Holm for any pre-set threshold above {{derived.H4.tip_threshold_holm|pct1}}, which leaves at least {{derived.H4.tip_k_min_holm|int}} of {{sample.n_sites|int}} sites below it. The H4 conclusion is for the four-hour shape, and the H5 slope result is window-sensitive (Section~\ref{sec:results:h5}).

\paragraph{External validity}The sample is campus-heavy and hourly, whereas commercial and industrial programs often settle on 15-minute data \citep{mathieu2011,coughlin2009}. The held-out year, 2017, uses the same {{sample.n_sites|int}} sites as the development year, so no claim extends to other sites, years or building stocks. The 2016 values are not results. These limits narrow the claim to a stress-tested comparison of information sets, within which each rule's exposure followed the hours it reads.

\paragraph{Future work}Programs with known event calendars would replace proxy events, and a randomized or staggered trial would supply real shaping behavior against which the injected increases can be compared \citep{todd2019}.
